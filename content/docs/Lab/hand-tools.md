# Hand Tools

I didn't need many tools when I started out because I was mostly breadboarding with Arduinos and sensors and so on. But as I started working more with old hardware I have accumulated more hand tools.

## Bending Tools

- **[**Narrow**](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/B0006N73BU) and [**Wide**](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/B07BGZ3LBK) Flat Pliers**. These are very useful for bending/straightening wires, and for straightening IC pins. I use these continuously when I'm working.
- **Grippy Long-Nose Pliers**. Every once in a while I need grippy pliers. I got some at Harbor Freight and they're bad but I haven't bothered replacing them yet.
- [**Lead Forming Tool**](https://www.amazon.com/CANADUINO-Bender-Resistors-Diode-Capacitor/dp/B089NZVB7S). When I'm building boards I like through-hole components to line up nicely.
- [**Engineer PA-24 Crimping Tool**](https://www.adafruit.com/product/5682). I had a hell of a time attaching Dupont and JST connectors until I got this crimping tool, and now it's a breeze and is no longer a point of anxiety. Highly, highly recommend.

## Grabbing Tools

- [**Cheap Tweezers**](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/B07RNVXXV1?th=1). I use cheap tweezers to fish parts out of containers and to hold/push on things when I'm soldering. Curved ones are also great for levering ICs out of breadboards. They get bent up and I bend them back.
- [**Rhino SW-11 Precision Straight Tweezers**](https://www.adafruit.com/product/3096). These are good Swiss tweezers that I only use to pick up SMD components. I find that cheap tweezers don't apply pressure evenly and cause parts to fly away.
- [**Jonard S-340 IC Puller**](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/B006C4BCAK). I much prefer levering ICs out with a screwdriver, but sometimes it's not possible to get in there and I have to pull them up from the top. These work but I end up bending pins 90% of the time so I'm not convinced this is actually a good tool.

## Screwdrivers

I like individual screwdrivers better than the kind where you can swap out tips. They take up much more space, but they allow me to reach for exactly the one I need and switch between them without a fiddly subtask.

- [**Wera Kraftform Micro Precision Screwdrivers**](https://www.amazon.com/Wera-Kraftform-Micro-Big-Pack/dp/B07X7JSBHG). I got this huge set as a gift and they're awesome. I have added more from this series, in larger sizes.
- **Big Screwdrivers**. I have a few big (i.e., normal-sized) screwdrivers for flat, Phillips, and Torx. I will probably end up needing more shapes and sizes of these.

## Cutting Tools

- [**Flush Wire Cutters**](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/B09R1JW8KF). I use one pair for copper wire and another pair for everthing else. They get dull pretty fast but they're cheap.
- **[Small](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/B005JVJDIA) and [Large](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/B073YG65N2) Wire Strippers**. I like these simple tools.
- [**Pink Box Cutter**](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/B09CM14CBH). Doing electronics means opening a lot of boxes. Pink so it's easy to find.
- Scissors
- Xacto Knife
- Cutting Mat


## Poking, Prying, and Scraping Tools

- [**Cheap Tweezers**](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/B07RNVXXV1?th=1). As above. They double as poking and levering tools.
- **[iFixit Jimmy](https://www.amazon.com/iFixit-Jimmy-Electronics-Opening-Tool/dp/B00NCFIVH4) and [Spudgers](https://www.amazon.com/Universal-Spudger-Opening-Tablets-MacBook/dp/B01DGNCNR0)**. Useful for prying, scraping, levering, etc., without causing a lot of damage.
- **Dental Picks**. I got a little set of tooth pokers and they're more useful than I expected.
- **Plastic Razor Blades**. Good tip from [IMSAI Guy](). These are great for scraping off stickers without scratching the underlying paint/plastic.

## Loser Hand Tools

Here are some hand tools and supplies that didn't work out.

- [**SN-28B Crimping Tool**](https://www.amazon.com/Glarks-Crimper-Connector-Crimping-AWG28-18/dp/B07VQ6YNSC). I don't like this tool because it crimps the conductor and insulation at the same time, and variations in insulation thickness means it's very easy for the crimping to be uneven. They also don't work at all with Dupont connectors because the insulation-crimping die is the wrong shape. The PS-24 above solves this by having both round and cardiod dies, and by crimping the conductor and insulation independently.
- [**Cheap Screwdrivers**]. I found that it's really easy to break cheap screwdrivers. I went through three sets from discount stores before putting the Wera set on my Christmas list.
- Harbor Freight wire stripper. This thing is miserable. I have regretted 100% of my purchases there.
